Approach

A method that survives contact with reality.

The same four moves underpin both practices — grounded in the evidence base of NYU's Executive Coaching and Organizational Consulting programme and the ICF Code of Ethics. The depth changes; the discipline does not.

  1. 01

    Contract

    Define the outcome, the boundaries, who else holds a stake, and what evidence of success will look like. Nothing starts before this is written down.

  2. 02

    Diagnose

    Interviews, data, assessment and observation — separating the presenting problem from the actual one. Most engagements move here.

  3. 03

    Work

    The sessions or the build. Deliberate practice, real stakes, live artefacts. Progress reviewed against the contract, not against activity.

  4. 04

    Embed

    Handover, capability transfer and a follow-up checkpoint. The measure is what still holds ninety days after I leave.

About Lara

I started on the commercial floor, not in an HR office.

My first real job with a number attached to it was retail banking at Byblos Bank in Beirut — housing loans, auto loans, credit cards, and brokering the bank's insurance products. Targets, customers, and the particular discomfort of a figure you have to make. That grounding is why my HR work has never been theoretical.

From there I spent three decades inside the people function, most of it at director level: Japan Tobacco International across the Middle East, North and West Africa; HR Director for the Levant at MSD through the Schering-Plough merger; HR Director Gulf at AstraZeneca; People & Culture Director for the GCC at Philip Morris International through its smoke-free transformation; and Human Capital Director for Carrefour's Central District in Dubai, supporting close to three thousand employees and a team of seven HR managers.

Coaching kept surfacing as the part of the work that actually moved people, so I went and got properly qualified in it — a Master of Science in Executive Coaching and Organizational Consulting from New York University, completed in 2025 with a 4.0 GPA, including 312 hours of ICF-accredited coach training and the ICF performance evaluation. I now hold the ICF Associate Certified Coach credential.

I work in English and Arabic, from Lebanon, with clients across the Gulf and the wider region. I take a deliberately small number of engagements so each one gets the attention it was contracted for.

Practical questions

Before you get in touch.

How much does coaching cost?

Engagements are priced as a whole rather than per session, because the outcome is what is being bought. Fees are confirmed after the chemistry call, once scope is clear, and differ depending on whether the work is self-funded or sponsored by an employer.

Is coaching confidential if my employer is paying?

Yes. Session content stays between us. Where an organisation sponsors the work we agree a three-way contract at the outset covering goals and how progress is reported — usually themes and progress against contracted outcomes, never session content. That agreement is written down before the first session, in line with the ICF Code of Ethics.

What does ICF ACC actually mean?

Associate Certified Coach is a credential from the International Coaching Federation, the profession's main global body. It requires completion of an accredited training programme, documented client coaching hours, a performance evaluation of recorded sessions, and a commitment to the ICF Code of Ethics. Mine was awarded in August 2026 through NYU's ICF Level 2 accredited master's programme and runs to August 2029.

Do you work virtually or in person?

Both. Coaching runs well virtually and most clients prefer it for consistency. Facilitation, leadership offsites and diagnostic interviews are usually stronger in person, and I travel across the Gulf and the Levant for those.

How do consulting engagements usually start?

Almost always with a scoped diagnostic of two to four weeks. It is deliberately small, gives you findings and costed options, and leaves you free to take the recommendation elsewhere. Committing to a full build before the problem is properly defined is how most HR programmes fail.

What languages do you work in?

Coaching, facilitation and consulting are delivered in English and Arabic.
